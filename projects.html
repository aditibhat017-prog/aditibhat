GaitMate

GaitMate is an early-stage wearable biosensing platform designed to support lateral ankle ligament rehabilitation. The system explores the use of sticker-based sensors placed across the ATFL, CFL, and PTFL to monitor strain patterns during recovery.

My role: I lead the development of GaitMate with a multidisciplinary team, translating an unmet rehabilitation need into a wearable sensing concept. My work has included designing a nine-location anatomical sensing framework, developing strain-analysis approaches using maximum strain, spatial gradients, and signal variability, and conducting clinical and customer discovery.

SafeTEA

SafeTEA is an aptamer-based platform developed through the UCSC iGEM team to investigate the capture of aflatoxin B1 and the development of biological approaches for reducing exposure to aflatoxins.

My role: As Undergraduate Research Team Lead, I led an 18-member interdisciplinary team spanning four wet-lab subteams and one dry-lab subteam. I also contributed directly to recombinant construct development through primer and plasmid design, Gibson Assembly, Golden Gate cloning, and optimization of fluorescence-based aptamer assays.

Gynosaur

Gynosaur is an exploratory women's health and digital-biomarker project investigating how longitudinal symptom and wearable data could be used to better understand patterns associated with endometriosis.

My role: I developed the project's digital-health framework to integrate menstrual symptoms, pain, sleep, activity, resting heart rate, heart-rate variability, and other wearable-derived physiological measures. The project explores how these variables could be analyzed together to identify personalized symptom patterns over time.

Digital Biomarkers for Cognitive Decline

This computational bioengineering project investigates whether wearable-derived activity measures contain useful information about cognitive performance in older adults using NHANES wearable, cognitive, and demographic data.

My role: I developed the analysis pipeline and harmonized the data into a 1,289-participant cohort. I performed feature engineering, regression, five-fold cross-validation, and cross-year generalization analyses. Mean activity was associated with cognitive performance (r = 0.174, p < 10−10), while wearable activity increased cross-validated model performance from R² = 0.379 to 0.386.

College Coursework

Fentanyl Minibinder

Worked with four other students to design a de novo protein minibinder targeting fentanyl and analogs using RFdiffusion and AlphaFold3, based on the µ-opioid receptor binding pocket. Applied rational design and biochemistry concepts to engineer high-affinity binding interactions. Developed a preventative therapeutic concept to neutralize fentanyl before receptor binding, with potential applications in overdose prevention.

Parkinson's Modeling in Organoids

Worked with three other students to create a Python program comparing spatial changes over time in organoids affected and unaffected by Parkinson's disease using data from Dr. Tal Sharf's lab. The program implemented average firing-rate plots, variance analysis, elbow graphs, and k-means clustering. Results showed decreased activity density in Parkinson's-represented clusters.
