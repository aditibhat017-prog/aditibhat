GaitMate

GaitMate is an early-stage wearable device platform designed to support lower-limb post-injury rehabilitation. Its initial application focuses on lateral ankle sprain recovery, using sticker-based sensors positioned across the anterior talofibular, calcaneofibular, and posterior talofibular ligaments to monitor strain patterns throughout recovery. GaitMate aims to bridge the gap between clinical visits by giving patients and clinicians a clearer picture of rehabilitation progress and changes in movement over time.

Aditi's Role

As founder and project lead, Aditi began GaitMate during an independent study in her final quarter at UC Santa Cruz, leading two other students through literature review, problem definition, and development of the initial wearable sensing concept. The project was partly inspired by her own experience recovering from injury and managing altered gait, particularly during COVID-19 when access to in-person clinical care was limited.

Since graduating, Aditi has continued developing GaitMate from an independent study into an early-stage venture while leading an expanded student team. Her work has included designing a nine-location anatomical sensing framework and developing strain-analysis approaches incorporating maximum strain, spatial gradients, and signal variability.

She has also led early business and customer development through the iGEM Venture Creation Lab, where the team placed second in its regional program. GaitMate has been presented through conferences and innovation events including the ASEE Zone IV Conference, UCSF QBI Hackathon, and 26th Annual University of California Bioengineering Symposium.

SafeTEA

SafeTEA, or Safe Targeted Extraction via Aptamers, was UCSC's 2025 iGEM project exploring an aptamer-based approach to reducing exposure to aflatoxins, toxic compounds produced by certain fungi that can contaminate food products. The team investigated DNA aptamers capable of binding aflatoxin B1 and developed an early capture-system concept for removing the toxin from aqueous products.

Aditi's Role

As captain and undergraduate research lead, Aditi led an eighteen-member interdisciplinary team through a full year of project development. She coordinated four wet-lab subteams and a dry-lab team, maintained communication between researchers and the Principal Investigator, and introduced Helix, an Agile-inspired project-management framework designed for student-led research teams.

Aditi also contributed across technical areas as project needs evolved, including experimental design and protocol adaptation, PCR, cloning, fluorescence assays, aptamer screening, and wiki development. Beyond the laboratory, she coordinated outreach and fundraising efforts that helped secure more than $80,000 in support for UCSC's iGEM program through engagement with companies, industry experts, potential sponsors, and younger science audiences.

At the iGEM Grand Jamboree in Paris, Aditi helped present the team's work and delivered a live talk on Helix and research-team organization. SafeTEA received a Silver Medal. Following the competition, she developed From Captain to Captain, a leadership resource for future student research leads, and later published and presented work on her leadership experience at the ASEE Zone IV Conference.

Gynosaur

Gynosaur is an early-stage women's health project exploring how wearable sensing and longitudinal symptom tracking could be combined to better understand reproductive health. Building on concepts first explored through GaitMate, the project investigates how wearable-derived measures such as heart rate, heart-rate variability, activity, sleep, and temperature could be analyzed alongside menstrual-cycle and symptom data.

Its initial work focuses on endometriosis, exploring whether patterns across pain, fatigue, bleeding, gastrointestinal symptoms, sleep, activity, and physiological measurements could help individuals better characterize symptom changes over time. The long-term vision is to investigate how similar approaches could support a broader range of reproductive-health conditions.

Digital Biomarkers for Cognitive Decline

This independent computational bioengineering project investigates whether wearable-derived activity measures provide meaningful information about cognitive performance in older adults. Using NHANES wearable, cognitive, and demographic data, Aditi developed a harmonized cohort of 1,289 adults aged 60 and older and created a Python-based analysis pipeline for feature engineering, regression, cross-validation, and cross-year generalization.

The analysis found that mean physical activity was associated with cognitive performance, while activity variability contributed comparatively little additional predictive information. Adding wearable activity measures produced a modest improvement over demographic-only models, highlighting both the potential and limitations of passive activity data as a digital biomarker for cognitive health.

College Coursework

Fentanyl Minibinder

Worked with four other students to design a de novo protein minibinder targeting fentanyl and analogs using RFdiffusion and AlphaFold3, based on the µ-opioid receptor binding pocket. Applied rational design and biochemistry concepts to engineer high-affinity binding interactions. Developed a preventative therapeutic concept to neutralize fentanyl before receptor binding, with potential applications in overdose prevention.

Parkinson's Modeling in Organoids

Worked with three other students to create a Python program comparing spatial changes over time in organoids affected and unaffected by Parkinson's disease using data from Dr. Tal Sharf's lab. The program implemented average firing-rate plots, variance analysis, elbow graphs, and k-means clustering. Results showed decreased activity density in Parkinson's-represented clusters.
